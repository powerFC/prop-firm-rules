FTMO vs FundedNext (2026)

Both run a two-phase forex/CFD evaluation with a static drawdown and a fixed daily loss limit — the two closest-matched 2-step plans in this comparison. The differences come down to phase targets, minimum trading days, and one restriction FTMO doesn't have.

What actually differs

The drawdown and daily-loss mechanics are identical on these two plans — both static at 10%, both a fixed 5% daily loss. The real differences are: FTMO's Phase 1 target is higher (10% vs. FundedNext's 8%) but needs one fewer minimum trading day (4 vs. 5); and FundedNext adds a 60-day inactivity restriction that FTMO's 2-Step doesn't carry. FTMO also offers a 1-Step alternative (trailing drawdown, a best-day consistency rule) that FundedNext's CFD line doesn't have a direct equivalent for in our data.

Which suits which trader

FundedNext suits you if…

  • You'd rather clear a lower 8% Phase 1 target, even at the cost of one extra minimum trading day.
  • You plan to trade at least occasionally throughout the evaluation and funded stage — the 60-day inactivity rule only bites if you go quiet.

FTMO suits you if…

  • You want the fewest minimum trading days to clear each phase.
  • You want zero inactivity risk, or you might need to pause trading for extended stretches.
  • You're also considering a 1-Step, trailing-drawdown path — FTMO has one, FundedNext's CFD line doesn't.

Frequently asked questions

Is FTMO or FundedNext's Phase 1 target easier?

FundedNext's Stellar 2-Step Phase 1 target is 8%, lower than FTMO's 2-Step Phase 1 target of 10%. FTMO requires fewer minimum trading days to get there, though — 4, versus FundedNext's 5.

Does FTMO have an inactivity rule like FundedNext?

Not in our verified data. FundedNext's Stellar 2-Step carries a 60-day inactivity restriction that can close the account; FTMO's 2-Step Evaluation carries no equivalent restriction.

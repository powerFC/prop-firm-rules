How FTMO's drawdown works

2-Step uses a static floor. Subtract 10% of initial simulated capital from that starting capital. On a $100,000 account the floor stays at $90,000 in both evaluation phases and on the subsequent 2-Step FTMO Account.

1-Step uses an end-of-day trailing floor. At midnight CE(S)T, FTMO subtracts 10% of initial simulated capital from the highest recorded midnight balance or initial capital, whichever is higher. On a $100,000 account the floor starts at $90,000. A $104,000 midnight balance raises it to $94,000, and a later drop in balance leaves the floor at $94,000. FTMO resets the floor if you withdraw a reward and receive a new 1-Step FTMO Account. See FTMO's Trading Objectives.

Current and legacy 1-Step consistency rules

For accounts initiated from orders created on or after 12 October 2026, or merged on or after that date, the best day's closed profit must be at most 50% of total closed profit. Losing days reduce that total. The legacy Best Day Rule counts only profitable days and applies to accounts directly initiated from earlier orders, subject to the merger rule.

The applicable 1-Step rule affects passing the challenge and reward eligibility on the subsequent FTMO Account. FTMO shows the result in Account MetriX.

Frequently asked questions

Is FTMO's max drawdown static or trailing?

The 2-Step maximum-loss floor stays at $90,000 on a $100,000 simulated account. The 1-Step floor starts at $90,000 and rises to $94,000 once the balance reaches $104,000 at the daily reset. A subsequent lower reset balance leaves that floor at $94,000.

Does FTMO have a consistency rule?

FTMO 1-Step caps the best day's closed profit at 50% of total closed profit, including losing days, for accounts initiated from orders created on or after 12 October 2026 or merged on or after that date. Accounts directly initiated from earlier orders use the legacy Best Day Rule, which counts profitable days only. The 2-Step Evaluation has no consistency rule.

Is the FTMO evaluation fee refundable?

On the 2-Step Evaluation, yes — it's refunded once you pass. On the 1-Step Evaluation, the fee is non-refundable, per our verified notes.
